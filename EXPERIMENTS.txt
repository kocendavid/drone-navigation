Roadmap: from measured movement to autonomous exploration
=========================================================
The user requested larger increments: repeated control, mapping and route
planning are now being combined into one mapped-goal navigation milestone.
See docs/MAPPED_NAVIGATION.txt and README.md. The first live map-goal flight
reached its destination on 2026-10-14. Frontier exploration and a faster profile
are now prepared; their live run remains pending.
Every stage has a question, predicted result, measured result and explanation.
The operator controls simulator changes, arming and flight-mode handovers.
All experiments remain simulated. A code/test result is distinct from a
completed flight experiment. Current status is recorded explicitly below.

0. Follow one command -- completed
Request a 0.5 m forward step and hold its endpoint.
Evidence: measured travel about 0.503 m, sideways 0.015 m, height 0.999 m.
Lessons: PX4 versus ROS roles, Offboard, NED/ENU, feedback, simulation timing.

1. Stop using sensed evidence -- earlier isolated lesson
Refuse a step with a nearby obstacle; stop an allowed step as it approaches;
then remove sensing and verify UNKNOWN stops further movement.
Measure trigger distance, stopping travel, sensor age and actual mode/height.
Lessons: perception latency, thresholds, feedback, confidence and failure modes.
Status: implementation, 68 tests and live perception check pass. Flight pending.

2. Run repeated bounded commands -- combined into persistent mapped controller
Replace one-shot restart choreography with one persistent controller that
accepts individual operator steps/turns while retaining the obstacle gate.
Run an out-and-back or square experiment; measure endpoint error and drift.
Lessons: state machines, command ownership, frames and repeatability.

3. Build a map while moving -- live pipeline verified
Transform validated LiDAR rays into occupied, observed-free and unknown cells.
Initially use Isaac world TF as known localization, aligned with PX4's origin.
Scan from multiple viewpoints and compare the map with the room geometry.
Measure map consistency, unknown area and coverage of observed space.
Lessons: coordinate transforms, ray tracing, occupancy and localization error.

4. Reach a goal through known space -- first live goal completed
Choose a goal beyond the box. Find a route through observed free space with
aircraft clearance; follow it using bounded movement and retain stop guards.
Measure goal error, minimum clearance and behavior when the route is blocked.
Lessons: path planning, footprint inflation, trajectory following and replanning.
Result: planned 6.52 m detour, REACHED, about 1-3 cm endpoint error, height
approximately 1 m, armed Offboard with no failsafe at endpoint verification.
Full trajectory/minimum flight clearance were not recorded in that first run.

5. Choose the next place to inspect -- current larger milestone
Detect boundaries between observed free and unknown space. Select a reachable
viewpoint, plan, move, scan and repeat until no useful reachable frontier remains.
Run the same policy in several differently arranged rooms.
Measure coverage versus simulated time, travel distance and failures.
Lessons: frontier exploration, information gathering and autonomous decisions.
Status: 42 navigation tests pass. Offline replay selected two viewpoints and
revealed 30.47 m² of hidden space. Updated live explorer awaits operator reload.
Scene suggestion: 2.2 m corridors, multiple corners and a dead end, preserving
the current floor/sensor/bridge. Current settings: cruise up to 0.5 m/s, slow
below 1 m, stop at 0.65 m, LiDAR near range 0.5 m, obstacle margin 0.8 m.

6. Challenge the assumptions
Add timing delays, dropped scans, noise, moved obstacles and localization drift.
Replace perfect world localization with an estimated pose when ready.
Measure degradation, recovery and whether stops occur before collisions.
Lessons: robustness, estimation, SLAM/localization and realistic evaluation.

7. Investigate learned exploration, if useful
Keep the geometric explorer as a baseline. Collect simulated runs and train
a policy to choose viewpoints or exploration actions. Test in unseen rooms
against the baseline with the same coverage/time/clearance metrics.
Lessons: datasets, rewards, training, generalization and honest comparisons.
Learning chooses exploration decisions; bounded flight control remains separate.
