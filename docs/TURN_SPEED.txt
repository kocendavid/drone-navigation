Turn speed and route smoothing — 20 October 2026

The route should spread direction changes over available known free space.
The total bend angle alone does not determine the speed it can maintain.
These results describe the offline reference model, not measured aircraft limits.

Current limits

Configured cruise is 1.2 m/s and vector acceleration is 0.25 m/s². The route
profile reserves 60% (0.15 m/s²) for lateral acceleration, leaving room for
tangential speed changes. Its continuous curve bound is:

    speed <= sqrt(0.15 * radius)

Radius (m)         1       2       4       6       9.6
Speed (m/s)      0.387   0.548   0.775   0.949    1.2

A smooth 90° turn of radius 10 m can keep cruise under this model. A sharp
15° vertex cannot: the direction changes over very little distance.

Unrounded vertices

The former policy assigned zero speed above 12° between adjacent segments.
It now combines measured polyline curvature with a 2 cm corner-blend budget.
For angle theta, an implicit tangent fillet whose radius is R deviates from
the vertex by R * (sec(theta/2) - 1). Its radius cannot exceed:

    R = 0.02 / (sec(theta/2) - 1)

The effective radius is the smaller of this bound and the sampled geometric
radius. Under the current model, an isolated unrounded kink can keep 1.2 m/s
only up to approximately 7.39°. Limits then decrease continuously:

Local kink       5°      10°      12°      15°      30°      45°      90°
Speed (m/s)     1.20     0.89     0.74     0.59     0.29     0.19     0.085

This is a modeling choice tied to the deviation budget, not evidence that the
aircraft must slow at 7.39° of total yaw. Curved routes have many small local
angles. Goal braking, short adjoining segments, tracking and obstacles can
reduce speed further. Tight unresolved corners retain the conservative
0.4 m approach zone and near-hover handling.

Yaw and handover

Yaw uses a chord about one second ahead (0.3–3 m), compensating some of the
bounded yaw reference's lag during steady curves. Facing error starts a smooth
speed-cap transition at 30° and reaches the configured 0.7 m/s cap at 60°.
It does not request zero translation speed. Yaw rate and acceleration remain
bounded by the configured 60°/s and 90°/s².

Abrupt travel-direction handovers still brake when reference or measured speed
exceeds 0.15 m/s and the onward direction has dot product below 0.7 with the
current travel direction: approximately 45.57°. This is an existing safety
guard for a discontinuous handover, not a maximum angle for an entire smooth
curve. Removing it needs moving-flight evidence and a validated replacement
that accounts for stopping space and actual velocity.

Route selection and replanning

A* retains its clearance and known-space center preference. The planner
compares shortcut tolerances of 2%, 8% and 15%, rounds each safely, and excludes
candidates costing more than 5% above the conservative candidate's integrated
center/clearance cost. Eligible candidates minimize:

    rest-to-rest estimated seconds
    + 0.25 * total speed variation / configured acceleration
    + (weighted clearance cost in metres - route length) / cruise

The travel estimate includes forward acceleration and backward braking bounds;
it excludes live tracking and sensor interventions. This searches a bounded
candidate family derived from the A* route, not all possible continuous routes.
Frontier destination ordering still uses planned geometric length.

Rounding requests a radius appropriate to cruise and the acceleration budget,
limited to 40% of each adjoining segment to avoid overlapping neighboring
curves. Candidate curves must pass every safe-mask segment check. A new tail
may use a cubic blend starting tangent to the committed prefix and ending
tangent to its retained tail. The prefix itself stays exact for asynchronous
rebasing. An unsafe or worse-scoring blend falls back to the original tail.
Same-goal replanning requires a 5% combined-score improvement to avoid churn.

Intermediate samples describe route geometry. They no longer discard the
unused distance of a control interval. An offline dense straight-line case
with 8 cm samples and a 0.1 s step previously reduced 1.2 m/s to 0.8 m/s;
the corrected follower keeps 1.2 m/s. Goal and tight-corner clamps remain.
Current-map motion and stopping-envelope audits still run on actual candidates.

Reproduction and flight evaluation

    python tools/analyze_turn_speed.py

Writes results/turn_speed/turn_speed.csv, summary.json and turn_speed.png using
the active navigation settings. The sweep compares sharp vertices and rounded
bends across angles and tangent-cut distances. It sends no flight commands.
tests/test_turn_planning.py covers continuous limits, arc sampling, control
step sizes, route scoring, blocked blends, exact prefix rebasing and obstacles.
The normal full suite also checks tracking lag and vector acceleration.

The navigator must reload before these source changes affect a running flight.
For moving evaluation, compare identical goals and map snapshots at the same
cruise. Record speed variation, low-speed duration, travel time, clearance,
tracking error, yaw error and each dominant speed limiter. The speed-decision
record now includes the upcoming local turn angle and effective radius.
Physical turn capability remains unverified until that PX4/Isaac evaluation.
